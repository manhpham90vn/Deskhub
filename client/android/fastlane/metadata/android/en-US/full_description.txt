Deskhub streams the screen of your Windows, macOS or Linux computer to your phone and lets you control it with ultra-low latency — built for gaming, not just remote work.

<b>Features</b>
• Live H.264 video stream with hardware decoding
• Hear what the remote computer is playing, in step with the picture
• Virtual trackpad: drag anywhere to move the mouse, tap to click, double-tap to right-click, hold and drag to select
• Pinch to zoom into the remote screen; a two-finger drag scrolls, or pans once you are zoomed in
• Full keyboard input, plus a hotkey bar for Esc, Tab, Enter, arrows, Del, Ctrl+C/Ctrl+V
• Open a real shell on the remote computer in its own window, with a key row for Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt and the arrows
• Send files to the computer — and take the files it sends back: photos land in Pictures/Deskhub, videos in Movies/Deskhub, everything else in Download/Deskhub, and a notification names what arrived
• Connect once, then choose what to open: its screen, a shell, or file transfer
• Remembers the computers you connect to, with their name and when you last connected, so the next connection is one tap
• Choose which display to watch when the computer is sharing more than one
• Adaptive bitrate and forward error correction keep the stream smooth on shaky Wi-Fi
• A session that loses the network reattaches by itself instead of ending
• Optional clipboard sync: plain text copied on one device can be pasted on the other
• Share this phone's screen the other way round, view-only, so a computer can watch it — with its sound on Android 10 and later

<b>How it works</b>
Install the free Deskhub app on the computer you want to reach (download from the project page) and press Share there. The quickest way in: press Show QR code on the computer, then Scan QR code in Deskhub on your phone — the computer is trusted and lets this phone in at once. Or type the computer's address and connect: compare the fingerprint Deskhub shows with the one on the computer's Devices page, trust it, and have the computer's owner press Approve under Connection requests — this phone keeps trying for two minutes. Once it is in, pick what to open: its screen, a shell, or file transfer. Works on your local network out of the box; for access over the Internet, pair it with Tailscale and connect via your Tailscale IP.

<b>Good to know</b>
Every session is encrypted end to end over QUIC/TLS — video, sound, keystrokes, files, clipboard and everything else. Access works like SSH: the computer only lets in a phone its owner has let in on purpose — by pressing Approve, by showing it the QR code, or by pasting its public key — and your phone remembers each computer's host key after you confirm it the first time; if that key ever changes, Deskhub refuses to connect. A phone can be removed from the computer at any time. There are no accounts and no servers of ours: your phone talks only to your own computer.

Sharing this phone's sound needs the permission Android labels <i>Microphone</i>, because that is what its playback-capture API is gated behind. Deskhub never records a microphone.

<b>Requirements</b>
• A computer running Windows 10 or later (x64), macOS 14 or later, or a current Linux desktop (glibc 2.35+), with Deskhub 9 or newer installed — free from the project page
• Both devices on the same network, or connected through Tailscale
